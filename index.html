Your next small steps

Testing with a purpose

Discuss symptoms, family history, medicines and previous results before choosing tests. Your clinician decides which pathology or imaging is appropriate, and how often. There is no standard broad hormone or biomarker panel in this concept.

Ask about expected benefits, false positives, overdiagnosis, out-of-pocket costs and the follow-up plan. Medicare eligibility and rebates depend on the service and clinical circumstances.

Know your screening options.

Australian population guidance, with room for your individual history.

Screening is for people without symptoms. New breast changes, abnormal bleeding or bowel symptoms need medical assessment; do not wait for screening. Family history, previous abnormal results and higher risk can change your pathway.

Care that grows with you.

Your needs change. Your care should make space for that.

Life stages are flexible. Pregnancy, menopause, medical history and gender-affirming care may change what is relevant. Screening depends on anatomy and individual risk as well as age.

Australian launch requirements

This is a product concept, not a clinically deployed service or a certification of legal compliance. Before handling real health data or delivering care, the business needs a documented clinical and legal review.

  • Clinical governance: engage appropriately registered Australian practitioners, verify registration, establish assessment, referrals, critical-result escalation, follow-up ownership and telehealth procedures. Contract with suitable accredited pathology providers.
  • Privacy: map obligations under the Privacy Act 1988 and Australian Privacy Principles. Health service providers holding health information are covered even when small. Include collection notices, appropriate consent, access and correction, security, retention and eligible data-breach response.
  • State and territory rules: assess health-records and privacy requirements in the jurisdictions served, including NSW, Victoria and the ACT. Establish record retention and complaints processes.
  • Software regulation: assess each intended purpose and claim against TGA medical-device rules. Diagnostic, treatment or monitoring features may require ARTG inclusion unless an exclusion or exemption applies. A disclaimer alone does not determine classification.
  • Advertising and consumer law: review claims against the National Law, Ahpra guidance and Australian Consumer Law; apply therapeutic-goods advertising rules where relevant. Avoid misleading benefit claims, clinical testimonials and encouraging unnecessary testing.
  • Commercial readiness: validate fees, refund terms, Medicare billing, practitioner indemnity, accessibility, incident management and vendor contracts. Do not claim Australian-only hosting without verifying hosting, backups, subprocessors and overseas disclosures.

PROPOSED VICTORIAN CARE PATHWAY

One connected care journey.

1

Clinician assessment

An appropriately registered practitioner assesses history, symptoms and risk, explains options and orders tests only when indicated.

2

Results explained, actions agreed

The proposed service would use suitable accredited pathology partners, clinician interpretation and a documented follow-up plan. Coaching supports agreed nutrition and lifestyle goals.

3

Follow-up with clear ownership

Before launch, establish responsibility for critical results, timely contact, referrals and rechecks. This prototype has no monitoring or emergency response.
